1. How well did each agent perform?

I graded each agent on four things I can check in the audit trail: (a) facts: does every number it used match a tool result from the database? (b) routing: did it call only the agents and tools the ticket needed (hand-offs, tool calls, tokens)? (c) rules: no money without a human, rent first, orders held behind unpaid bills, discount limits. (d) finish: did the requester get a correct answer and the ticket the right status? Facts were never the problem: in all four runs no agent invented a number, and cash always ended at the database's $160.00. Routing, rules, and finish are where runs differed, and each failure led to a fix (section "Problems found and fixed").

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

  • Ticket 101 in one loop. One agent with check_stock_by_sku_size, list_open_invoices, list_leases, get_cash_balance, request_invoice_payment, and add_ticket_note could go from "S = 0" to "invoice 501 blocks it" to "prepare $840.00 and reply to Tauhid" without three hand-offs. The run 2 misunderstanding ("confirm" instead of "prepare") cannot happen when one agent holds the whole context.
  • Less repeated work. Every delegated agent starts cold. In the final run get_ticket and get_shop_date each ran 6 times for 3 tickets, 103 ran the same two quotes twice, and the run used 44 model calls, 55 tool calls, and about 115,000 tokens. Ticket 101 alone used 45,393 tokens.
  • 102 and 103 are straight lines. 102 is verify → request; 103 is price → stock → reply. Neither needed a second opinion.
  • Easier to debug. The five problems above came from rules spread over four prompt files and from routing that changed from run to run. One prompt is one place to fix.
  • Safety would not suffer. What protects the shop (agents cannot execute payments, amounts come from the database, rent is held back, a customer order needs a reply, the discount policy is enforced by the quote tool) lives in the MCP server and the approve route, not in the agent split. The team adds least privilege (Facilities cannot request payments) and short role prompts, which matter more as the shop grows.

5. Three new problems it cannot solve, and what it would need

A. Selling the hoodies and taking the club's money

If the Yale AI Club accepts the 103 quote and pays $1,044.00 (or $464.00 for the 8), nothing can record it: no tool adds money to cash_accounts, lowers inventory when items leave, or adds stock when the 101 reprint arrives. Cash only goes down, which is why ticket 103 could not fund its own restock and ticket 101 cannot be fulfilled inside the system. Needed: MCP tools record_sale(sku, size, qty, unit_price) (new sales table, lowers inventory, raises checking), take_deposit(ticket_id, amount), and receive_shipment(po_or_invoice_id); a Sales agent for point of sale, deposits, and refunds, with refunds needing human approval.

B. Talking to the outside world

Tickets exist only because they were already in the database, and replies exist only as ticket notes: Tauhid and the club never receive anything, and nobody tells Bulldog Print Co to start the reprint after invoice 501 is paid. Needed: an inbox connector with read_inbox and create_ticket, send_email(to, body) gated by human review, and place_vendor_order(vendor_id, po); an Intake agent that turns emails into tickets and screens spoofed senders like 4B.

C. Planning cash before it runs out

After the run checking is $160.00 and October rent ($2,400.00) is due 2026-10-02. The team sees the balance but cannot forecast it: there is no sales history, no schedule of upcoming bills beyond rent, and the shop date never advances, so it cannot say whether the shop will make rent or when the 5-day reprint lands. Needed: list_upcoming_obligations(days) (rent, invoices, purchase orders by due date), forecast_cash(days) from sales history, and advance_shop_date for simulations; a Finance/Planning agent that runs these weekly and recommends pricing, deposits, or delaying purchases, with any borrowing left to a person.
