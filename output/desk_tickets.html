Reflection

Evidence: the Expected/Actual sections on the ticket tabs, the Cash tab, and output/audit_trail.json (clean runs t101-0c6826ff, t102-08fbf1ce, t103-3283da3e; first attempts t102-802de7c2 and t103-8cb082a8).

1. How well did each agent perform?

I graded each agent on four things I can check in the audit trail: (a) facts: did every number it used match the database (the MCP tool output)? (b) routing: did it call only the agents and tools the ticket needed, measured by hand-offs, tool calls, and tokens? (c) rules: did it follow the shop rules (no money without a human, rent first, hold orders behind unpaid bills, discount limits)? (d) finish: did the requester end up with an answer and the ticket with the right status? Facts were never the problem; no agent invented a number in any run, and the cash ended exactly at the database's $160.00. The differences were in routing and finish.

2. Actual vs. expected, ticket by ticket

  • Ticket 101. Plan: boss → inventory → accounting, then boss → customer_service. Actual: boss → inventory → accounting, then boss → accounting again, and no Customer Service. The first agent, the hand-off between specialists, the $840.00 request, and the end state ($3,400.00 → $2,560.00, resolved) matched. Two differences: the inventory → accounting hand-off lost the intent (Inventory asked "is there a request?" instead of "prepare one"), and the reply to the customer was skipped. list_vendors, which I expected for a restock date, never ran.
  • Ticket 102. Plan: boss → facilities → accounting with a $2,400.00 rent request. Actual: identical in the clean run, same tools, $2,560.00 → $160.00, lease next_due moved to 2026-10-02. The difference was the first attempt, where Facilities stopped at "amount cannot be verified." The plan assumed the notice and the lease would be compared on name and date, and I had not noticed that my own prompt also demanded an amount the email never gives.
  • Ticket 103. Plan: boss → accounting → inventory, then boss → customer_service; a 12-unit order refused by the server for cash; resolved with the quote. Actual: the same three hand-offs and the same $1,044.00 quote, resolved with cash unchanged at $160.00. But the restock was never attempted (Accounting forbade it), so the server-side cash refusal I planned to show never happened. In the first attempt the Boss left the ticket open waiting for the club's answer, which my plan counted as resolved.

Overall, the who-calls-whom part of the plan held on all three tickets (Facilities skipped on 101 and 103; Inventory and Customer Service skipped on 102). What the plan got wrong was how literally agents read their prompts (102, 103 first attempts) and how much meaning survives a hand-off (101).

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

  • Ticket 101 would not have needed three hand-offs. One agent with check_stock_by_sku_size, list_open_invoices, get_cash_balance, and request_invoice_payment could go from "S = 0" to "invoice 501 is the blocker" to "prepare $840.00" in one loop. The misunderstanding between Inventory and Accounting cannot happen when the same agent holds the whole context.
  • Less repeated work. Every delegated agent starts cold, so across the three clean runs get_ticket ran 9 times and get_shop_date 8 times for 3 tickets, and the run used 40 model calls and about 97,000 tokens. A single agent would read the ticket and date once.
  • Ticket 102 and 103 are straight lines. 102 is verify → request; 103 is price → stock → reply. Neither needed a second opinion, so the delegation added latency without adding judgment.
  • Easier to debug and to keep consistent. Both first-attempt failures came from rules spread across two prompt files (facilities.md, boss.md), and the 101 Customer Service skip came from the Boss's routing. One prompt is one place to fix.
  • The safety would not have suffered. The rules that actually protect the cash (agents cannot execute payments, amounts come from the database, purchases must fit after pending bills) live in the MCP server and the approve route, not in the agent split. What the team did add is least privilege (Facilities cannot request a payment) and short role prompts. That matters more as the shop grows than it did for three tickets.

4. Three new problems this team can already solve

A. A student orders 2 Yale Caps

A customer_order for CC-HAT-BLUE, size OS. Boss → Inventory (check_stock_by_sku_size: 40 on hand, Aisle C, no open invoice) → Boss → Customer Service (get_sku_pricing: $22.00 each) writes the reply and saves it with add_ticket_note. No money moves, so it resolves without approval; this is ticket 101 without the blocker.

B. A fake rent notice

An email from "Elm City Property Mgmt" saying $2,600 rent is due tomorrow. Facilities runs get_lease_rent_due for lease 1: landlord is Elm City Properties, rent $2,400.00, next due 2026-10-02 after our September payment. Name, amount, and date all mismatch, so under its rule it does not hand the notice to Accounting and the Boss leaves it open for a human. Ticket 102 showed the verification step works; this case is what it is for. Even if an agent tried, request_rent_payment takes the amount from the lease, not from the email.

C. Restock the Crest Mug

CC-MUG-CREST shows 0 on hand. Inventory uses list_vendors to pick Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3 lead days), Accounting checks get_cash_balance ($160.00), and request_restock_purchase prices 20 mugs at 20 × $3.50 = $70.00, which fits, with arrival on shop date + 3. A human approves it on the board. The same tool would refuse 12 hoodies ($264.00) for ticket 103 right now, so the team can tell which restock is affordable.

5. Three new problems it cannot solve, and what it would need

A. Selling the hoodies and taking the club's money

If the Yale AI Club accepts the 103 quote and pays $1,044.00, nothing can record it: no tool adds money to cash_accounts, lowers inventory when items leave, or adds stock when a reprint arrives. Cash only goes down in this system, which is why ticket 103 could not fund its own restock. Needed: MCP tools record_sale(sku, size, qty, unit_price) (writes a new sales table, lowers inventory, raises checking), take_deposit(ticket_id, amount), and receive_shipment(po_id); a Sales agent (point of sale, deposits, refunds) with human approval for refunds.

B. Talking to the outside world

Tickets only exist because they were already in the database, and replies only exist as ticket notes; Tauhid and the club never actually receive anything, and nobody tells Bulldog Print Co to start the reprint after invoice 501 is paid. Needed: an inbox/email connector with read_inbox and create_ticket, send_email(to, body) gated by human review, and place_vendor_order(vendor_id, po); an Intake agent that turns emails into tickets (and screens spoofed senders like problem 4B) and an outbound step for Customer Service.

C. Planning cash before it runs out

After the three tickets checking is $160.00, and October rent ($2,400.00) is due 2026-10-02. The team can see the balance but cannot forecast it: there is no sales history, no list of upcoming bills, and the shop date never advances, so it cannot say whether the shop will make rent or when the 5-day reprint lands. Needed: tools list_upcoming_obligations(days) (rent, invoices, POs by due date), forecast_cash(days) from sales history, and advance_shop_date for simulations; a Finance/Planning agent that runs these scenarios weekly and recommends actions such as pricing, deposits, or delaying a purchase, with any borrowing left to a human.
